Kingdom
of None

A turn-based roguelite card RPG · in development in Godot 4

Tomás Becerra · narrative & systems design

Four peoples share a continent, and under it runs a mineral called the Veth. Wear a ring set with it, look into a mirror and say: I look good today. You will feel that it is true.

The stone seems to agree with whoever holds it. It also carries heat, and under the right conditions it makes the wings of dead griffins fly. With those wings the Hilläru, the last people to arrive and the fewest in number, took the sky, and from the sky they took the continent: its tribute, its archives, its law. The same process, with a different measure of Veth, makes gunpowder. The stone that gave them their power also makes the weapon that can bring them down.

Everyone needs the Veth, and no two peoples agree on what it is. For the Orthal of the Father it is a voice that demands labour; for the Orthal of the Mother, a stone that does not speak. The Yrei hear in it a dialect of the sea. The Pherion guilds put it to work. The Hilläru treat it as engineering and decline to give it a theology. Each reading has its own politics, and every one of them is a reason to fight.

Even the world's own records describe the Veth in two vocabularies that cannot both be true. In one, it perceives, waits and chooses. In the other, it chooses nothing and only registers what the world was always able to become. If the stone that agrees with you has no will of its own, whose will has been moving the continent?

Kingdom of None does not answer. It is a game, and it asks the question where a game can: in the rules. What you are, what you carry and what you draw decide what you can do. Whether the choice that follows is yours is the question the game keeps asking.

from a card roguelite to a world

The game

Kingdom of None started as a 2D pixel-art card roguelite with fixed dungeons. It grew into a semi-open 3D world: thirty-six territories to cross, settlements to walk into, and people whose interests pull against each other. Its story is told through their errands. The player does not direct it and is not its hero; the player watches it from inside, and takes part.

settlements

Places you walk into

Settlements are 3D spaces composed for the player's camera, never for the aerial view. The player walks their streets, talks to their people and uses their services: the smithy, the inn, the gate, the lodging and the mission board.

the company

A company, not a hero

The player recruits characters from the twelve archetypes and sends them out on runs. They level up, carry trauma and die. The company keeps what it learned: its missions, its journal and the lore it gathered.

missions

The mission board

Every settlement offers three main missions and two rotating tasks. One main mission conquers the settlement and opens the next difficulty; at least one sends the player into a neighbouring province. Missions are official errands for the settlement's government, so each one serves some interests at the expense of others.

events

Events, situations, threads

On the road, events remember the player across runs, branch, and offer options only some bodies can take: reading what a stranger leaves unsaid takes Cosmopolitanism. Situations are smaller and forget. Named threads tie events together across provinces in the journal.

reputation

Nobody loves everyone

Reputation runs on sixteen counters, the four peoples and their twelve subfactions, each with five levels named in its own culture's words. Since missions set factions against each other, a perfect standing with all of them is out of reach. A good standing with everyone carries no penalty.

biomes and travel

The land bites back

Every step between nodes costs travel energy, and thirty-four of the thirty-six territories press on the traveller with one or more of fourteen hostilities, from cold to thin air. A character who runs out of energy faints and wakes at the refuge with one more trauma and half the bag. Nobody gets stranded.

economy and crafting

Scarce gold, honest recipes

Gold is meant to be scarce and worth finding. Prices derive from recipes, so buying finished goods costs more than buying ingredients, and crafting from loot costs least, by construction. Each province's smithy forges its own culture's equipment; universal pieces can be made anywhere.

the run map · october 2026

The continent

Four peoples, twelve provinces, thirty-six territories. Each province is played at three difficulties, D1 to D3. Provinces open in three blocks, and a block opens only after the previous one is complete in all four peoples, so every run pushes the player across cultures. Choose a settlement on the map.

a history of the continent · 70000 AR to 722 DR

The Seld archive

The history of the world as the Hilläru keep it, from the first myths to the year the game takes place. The dates are approximate. AR and DR count backwards and forwards from year 0, the Refoundation, when the Hilläru dominion began.

combat system

The engine

Kingdom of None is a card game in shape only. Its engine sits one layer below the surface, where three different objects work together. Each answers one question: who the body is, what it carries, and what the hand can do this turn.

the body

Archetype moves

Twelve archetypes, one per subfaction. Each has six fixed stats and nine signature moves: four common, three special, two elite. The moves are the subfaction's cultural property, and each body works a resource no item can grant: the Wanderer's cycle, the Okrosh's fury, the Reader's echo.

what it carries

Equipment cards

Over the body, six slots. Each item adds its own combat cards to the deck, gated by stats. No item is locked to a faction; a body raised far from it simply reads it less deeply.

what lands

Impacts

Combat cards do not strike. They drop impacts into a separate lane of the hand: cutting, piercing, blunt, control, block, dodge. Impacts sit outside the deck, the discard and the reshuffle. The body sets up; the equipment lands.

the impact lane, after Tear

A Sharp Knife puts two combat cards in the deck. Playing Tear drops three cutting impacts into the hand, where archetype moves can then change them before they land.

The making

The game is built and playable: combat, equipment, events, missions, reputation, crafting, the 3D run map and its settlements. What is still in progress: the enemy response system, the combat presentation and the narrative content of the later provinces.

Art and sound
Concept and card art generated with Midjourney under Tomás's direction. 3D settlements built from purchased low-poly asset packs. Music generated with Suno; sound effects from free libraries.

On tools

Kingdom of None was directed by one person and built with machines: a language model for the code and for drafts, an image model for the art, a music model for the score. None of them decides what the game is. Direction is the work: choosing, rejecting, rewriting, and knowing why. It is said here plainly because it is true, and because the work should be judged for what it is.

Codex

Everything made so far that already has its art: the archetypes and their 108 moves, the equipment of the first two blocks with its combat cards, and the animated enemies of the first four provinces. A script reads the game’s data files and art folders and writes this appendix, so it grows with the game. Costs, damage and hit points are alpha values and will move with the next combat revision.
